# Executive Technical Brief

## Decision in one sentence
SCILLA PASSIVE has reached a credible **simulation-evidence** state for industrial review, but not a field-performance state: the architecture's value survives hard simulation, while the optimizer moat does not.

## What is being proposed
Not a new passive-radar principle. Not a radar replacement.

**SCILLA PASSIVE EVIDENCE** is a candidate integration layer that begins with an existing uncertain maritime cue and asks whether an upcoming non-cooperative merchant-radar illumination can provide a valid passive second observation before an active/EO revisit is consumed.

## Strongest current evidence
- 300 paired nominal worlds per policy.
- Median final position error: **351.5 m no-passive vs 19.2 m metrology-conditioned passive** [SIMULATED].
- 95% bootstrap CI for passive median: **16.9-21.8 m** [DERIVED FROM SIMULATION].
- 54 physics robustness cells, 30 worlds/cell.
- Passive architecture beats no-passive in **94.4%** of cells.
- Three failure cells are preserved, concentrated in sparse-donor / low-RCS / high-clutter regimes.

## Most important negative result
The transparent metrology-conditioned EIG policy does **not** robustly beat a simple shortest-pulse baseline. In the paired nominal set it wins only **49.7%** of worlds and has a paired median difference of **0.0 m**.

### Consequence
Do not sell an optimizer. Sell/test the **measurement architecture and integration logic**.

## Missing evidence
- real RF dynamic range;
- merchant-radar waveform/reference extraction;
- sea clutter and multipath;
- emitter association reliability;
- bistatic RCS distribution;
- field geolocation accuracy;
- customer economics / willingness to integrate.

## Acquisition-relevant next step
A leader with existing RF labs, radar payloads or maritime datasets can close the dominant gap at far lower marginal cost than a capital-constrained independent programme. The diligence object is therefore this reproducible corpus plus a partner-sponsored falsification protocol, not a claim that the technology is already operational.
